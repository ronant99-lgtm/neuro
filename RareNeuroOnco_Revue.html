Clinical Signal 2025–2026

ATRT prognosis has improved substantially over the past decade, driven primarily by intensified regimens incorporating HDCT/PBSC and early focal RT — 4y EFS reaching ~37–40% in ACNS0333, compared to <5% historically in children <3 years. The molecular subgrouping (SHH · TYR · MYC) defined by DNA methylation profiling is now the central framework: SHH-subgroup carries the best prognosis, while MYC-subgroup remains the most aggressive. SIOPE ATRT01 (EU) is building treatment arms around this stratification.

Early RT remains critical — most failures are local within the first 2 years, and postponing RT beyond HDCT is associated with inferior LC. The key question for 2026 is whether EZH2 inhibition (tazemetostat) or BET-bromodomain inhibitors can meaningfully extend survival in relapsed or high-risk MYC-subgroup disease, where outcomes remain dismal.

Clinical Signal 2025–2026

ETMR remains one of the most lethal pediatric CNS tumors, with median survival <12–18 months in most historical series regardless of treatment intensity. The C19MC amplification/TTYH1 fusion that defines the entity is present in ~90% of cases and is now required for diagnosis per WHO 2021; LIN28A immunohistochemistry serves as a practical surrogate marker.

The clearest emerging signal is the DICER1-mutant ETMR-like subset of infantile cerebellar embryonal tumors, which appears to carry meaningfully better outcomes and may warrant distinct treatment consideration. For the C19MC-amplified majority, there is no established standard beyond HDCT frameworks (Head Start 4, SJYC07 paradigm). No targeted therapy has demonstrated efficacy. Enrollment in any available embryonal tumor trial remains the highest priority for new diagnoses.

Clinical Signal 2025–2026

Pineoblastoma molecular subgrouping (miRNA-1, miRNA-2, MYC/FOXR2, PPTID, RB1-assoc.) is now well-established in the research setting but has not yet been formally integrated into WHO classification or prospective trial stratification. This remains the dominant unmet need: outcomes diverge substantially by subgroup, yet treatment remains uniformly high-intensity.

The critical practical point from ACNS0334 data is sobering: all pineoblastoma patients treated in infancy expired within 2 years regardless of RT strategy. For children ≥3 years, the SJMB03/ACNS0332 HR-MB paradigm (CSI 23.4–36 Gy + IFRT 54 Gy) remains the best available framework with 5y OS 50–65%. DICER1 testing is mandatory at diagnosis as it may identify a distinct, more favorable subset.

Clinical Signal 2025–2026

GCT/NGGCT management is undergoing its most significant prospective refinement in a decade through two concurrent COG trials. For germinomas, ACNS2321 (recruiting since October 2024) directly tests whether WVI can be reduced to 12 Gy in CR patients — a meaningful question given that ACNS1123 already demonstrated 18 Gy WVI + 12 Gy boost is safe and effective (3y EFS 88%). The 12 Gy arm would represent the lowest published WVI dose in a prospective trial.

For NGGCT, the defining lesson of the past cycle is unambiguous: CSI cannot be replaced by WVRT — ACNS1123 closed early after 12% isolated spinal failures in the WVRT arm. ACNS2021 now tests whether 30.6 Gy CSI (vs 36 Gy) is sufficient for M0 disease, a more cautious de-escalation hypothesis. AFP normalization post-induction and second-look surgery for persistent marker elevation remain critical decision points in NGGCT management.

Clinical Signal 2025–2026

Ependymoma management is being transformed by molecular stratification. The central paradigm shift: PFB subgroup (excellent prognosis, ~5y EFS >85%) may tolerate de-escalated RT, while RELA fusion (supratentorial) and PFA with 1q gain carry the worst outcomes and may warrant intensification. ACNS1123 (COG) is the first prospective trial to assign RT dose based on molecular subgroup, with proton therapy mandated as the preferred modality.

The other dominant theme is the role of chemotherapy: ACNS0831 results (closed 2019, reported 2025–2026) will finally answer whether maintenance chemotherapy after RT improves EFS — a question debated for 20 years. The current signal from retrospective series suggests chemo benefit is limited to STR cases. For infants, ACNS0121 established that PORT at 12–18 months achieves EFS comparable to older children — a practice-changing result that moved away from chemo-first delay strategies.
