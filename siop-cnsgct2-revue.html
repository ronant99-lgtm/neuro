Diagnosis, Staging & Diagnostic Grouping

SIOP CNS GCT II · ESCP 2021 · Markers · Imaging · IHC · Dissemination
Tumour Markers — European Thresholds
MarkerThreshold (EU)SignificanceHalf-lifeNotes
AFP (alpha-fetoprotein) >25 ng/ml
serum and/or CSF		 Diagnostic of secreting malignant NGGCT — biopsy not mandatory 5–7 days Normal in children <2y; produced by YST and EC components. AFP >1000 ng/ml = high-risk NGGCT.
HCG (total β-HCG) >50 IU/L
serum and/or CSF		 Diagnostic of secreting malignant NGGCT — biopsy not mandatory 12–36 h Modest elevation (50–200 IU/L) may indicate syncytiotrophoblastic cells within germinoma. Method must detect β-free subunits.
AFP unit conversion: ng/ml × 0.83 = kU/L (IU/ml). After birth, AFP falls to normal range by age 2y — interpret elevated AFP in infants with caution.
HCG measurement method: Assay must detect total HCG including free β-subunits — intact HCG-only methods are insufficient for GCT. Half-life is only 12–36h: sample promptly.
Bifocal exception (marker-negative): Age >8–10y + DI + bifocal (pineal + suprasellar) + negative markers → treat as germinoma without biopsy. But note: 3.4% may be NGGCT (Kanamori 2021) — maintain low threshold for biopsy if response atypical.

Spine MRI Full spine · pre-LP & surgery
T1W+Gd sagittal whole spine (≤3mm, gap <0.5mm) + axial at suspicious areas. Must include lower dural sac. Perform BEFORE lumbar puncture and surgery.
Timing Rules
Post-operative MRI: Must be performed within 24–48h of surgery (and no later than 72h) to avoid non-specific enhancement artefact.
Spinal MRI: Ideally BEFORE lumbar puncture AND before surgery. If pre-operative MRI not obtained, perform as soon as feasible post-operatively.

Diagnostic Grouping — Germinoma vs NGGCT
Germinoma — Criteria
Biopsy-confirmed germinoma Histology
KIT+, POU5F1(OCT3/4)+, NANOG+, PLAP+/−, SOX2−. Strong lymphocytic infiltration — sparse malignant cells may require full IHC panel. Syncytiotrophoblastic cells may cause mild HCG elevation.
Bifocal marker-negative Clinical diagnosis
Age >8–10y + central DI + pineal and suprasellar masses + AFP ≤25 ng/ml + HCG ≤50 IU/L → treat as germinoma. Low threshold for biopsy if doubt (3.4% may be NGGCT — Kanamori 2021).
NGGCT — Criteria
Secreting NGGCT No biopsy needed
AFP >25 ng/ml and/or HCG >50 IU/L (serum and/or CSF) + typical radiology → NGGCT diagnosis, treatment may start without biopsy (80% sensitivity in serum, >60% in CSF).
Non-secreting NGGCT Biopsy required
Histological CHC, YST, and/or EC confirm NGGCT. MMGCT containing ≥1 of YST/EC/CHC treated as NGGCT. MMGCT with teratoma + germinoma only → treat as germinoma.
Dissemination Grouping
Localised vs Metastatic
M0 — Localised Including bifocal
Single pineal, single suprasellar, or bifocal (pineal + suprasellar). Contiguous ventricular/parenchymal extension may be acceptable — discuss with expert team. CSF cytology negative.
M+ — Metastatic Full neuroaxis
Radiological and/or cytological dissemination beyond primary site. ~20% of germinomas are M+ at diagnosis. Intraventricular extension documented by neurosurgeon during ETV should trigger M+ staging consideration.

SOX2 distinguishes EC (positive) from germinoma (negative). Gly3 + SALL4 useful for detecting small YST islands. CD30 rules in/out EC. Germinoma-EC distinction critical as treatment is identical (germinoma pathway for EC alone is not standard).
MMGCT: Search intensively for malignant GCT foci within teratomas — small YST/EC islands change treatment entirely. Germinoma + teratoma MMGCT → treat as germinoma. Any YST/EC/CHC → treat as NGGCT.
Initial Management Algorithm
Severe RICP (Emergency)
Emergency CT/MRI → collect CSF cytology + markers during CSF diversion → ETV preferred over EVD over shunt → serum markers immediately post-op → brain + spine MRI ideally within 24h → repeat LP for CSF cytology once RICP controlled.
RICP Stable
Brain + spine MRI + serum markers BEFORE surgery → CSF cytology + markers during diversion procedure → biopsy if necessary during same procedure → repeat LP when safe.
Normal ICP — Elective
Brain + spine MRI + serum markers → CSF via lumbar puncture (preferred over ventricular) for markers AND cytology → biopsy if markers non-diagnostic.
Biopsy Strategy
Biopsy NOT required when: AFP >25 ng/ml and/or HCG >50 IU/L (serum or CSF), OR bifocal disease in age >8–10y with DI + negative markers.
ApproachIndicationNotes
Endoscopic biopsy (ETV combined)During CSF diversion for hydrocephalusETV first to control ICP, then biopsy. May reveal lesions not seen on MRI → upstaging. ETV success 70–90%.
Stereotactic biopsySmall, deep-seated lesions — gold standardDiagnostic yield >95%, low complication rate. Requires separate procedure from ETV.
Open surgery / resectionSuspected teratoma, mass effect on visual pathwaysAvoid for other GCT subtypes. Upfront resection generally discouraged.
CSF Diversion — Preference Order
ETV > External ventricular drain (EVD) > Permanent ventriculoperitoneal shunt. GCT is very chemosensitive: EVD expected to be temporary. Lumbar CSF preferred over ventricular CSF for cytology (if safe).

Localised Germinoma — First-Line Treatment

SIOP CNS GCT II · Non-metastatic (M0) · Includes bifocal · ChemoRT strategy
Eligibility
Localised Germinoma Criteria
Disease
M0 germinoma — single-site (pineal OR suprasellar) or bifocal (pineal + suprasellar). Bifocal treated as non-metastatic. AFP ≤25 ng/ml AND HCG ≤50 IU/L (serum + CSF) or biopsy-confirmed.
Age
All ages — special chemotherapy dose considerations for <1y (<10 kg: per kg dosing) and very young (<6y, chemotherapy-only approach may be attempted to defer RT).
Staging
Complete staging required: brain + spine MRI, serum AND CSF markers, CSF cytology (lumbar preferred). Classify as M0 before treatment starts.

MRI head with/without contrast + serum markers (+ CSF markers if doubt). Classify response as CR, PR, or SD before determining RT.

SD — Suspected teratoma or no response Consider resection first
If SD with imaging suggestive of teratoma → resection before RT. If non-viable or pure germinoma on pathology → treat as PR (WVI+TB 24 Gy + 16 Gy boost). If TER + GER → WVI+TB 24 Gy + 30 Gy boost (total 54 Gy to gross disease).
Residual disease post-RT: Residual tumour after chemo does NOT adversely affect prognosis in germinoma (unlike other brain tumours) — watch-and-wait for small residuals. Surgery for bulky residuals only if teratoma/viable elements suspected.
3y EFS: Preliminary SIOP CNS GCT II data: 96% at 3y for all localised germinoma. SIOP CNS GCT 96 (n=190): 5y EFS/OS >90% with WVI 24 Gy + 16 Gy boost.

Metastatic Germinoma — First-Line Treatment

M+ at diagnosis · ~20% of germinomas · CSI-only approach · European standard
Treatment Rationale

Metastatic germinomas are treated with CSI alone without neoadjuvant chemotherapy, in contrast to the localised approach. The chemotherapy-first strategy used for localised disease (to spare radiation volume) does not apply when CSI is already mandatory due to dissemination. Outcomes remain excellent — comparable to localised disease.

If there will be a delay in delivering CSI (e.g. logistical), a short period of "holding" chemotherapy (carboplatin/etoposide or vinblastine) may be considered to prevent further disease-related morbidity.

Bridging Options — Pre-CSI only
Carboplatin + etoposide (standard doses), OR vinblastine monotherapy (Murray 2021 — used during COVID-19 delays). These are holding regimens only — not definitive treatment. Discuss with national GCT team.
Incompletely Staged Disease
Incompletely staged germinoma (incomplete spine MRI, no CSF cytology) → treat as metastatic with CSI + boost to be safe.

Outcomes SR

Treatment Rationale

The 5y EFS of only 32% for high-risk patients with standard PEI in SIOP CNS GCT 96 justifies dose intensification. HD-PEI + SCT was well tolerated in SIOP CNS GCT II with no serious adverse events reported. However, evidence remains insufficient to definitively recommend HD-PEI over standard PEI given the small patient numbers. Clinicians may offer HD-PEI + SCT with appropriate consent.

Teratoma — Mature & Immature

Surgical treatment · Chemo/RT resistance · Growing teratoma syndrome
Treatment Principles
Teratoma — Surgery is the Only Curative Option
Mature teratoma (MT) Surgery = complete resection
Complete surgical resection is the treatment of choice. Neither chemotherapy nor radiotherapy is effective for MT. Leaving large residues risks de-differentiation back to malignant GCT or somatic sarcomatous transformation (dismal prognosis).
Immature teratoma (IT) Individualized approach
Complete resection first choice. Rare cases of response to chemotherapy reported. RT may be considered for non-resectable residual or progression. Risk of malignant transformation — close surveillance mandatory.
RT for teratoma Only if unresectable
Consider RT for non-resectable residual disease and/or progression uncontrollable by repeated surgery. Not standard first-line treatment.
Growing Teratoma Syndrome (GTS)
Growing Teratoma Syndrome: Malignant NGGCT differentiates into mature teratoma elements — tumour increases in size despite chemotherapy with marker normalisation. Surgical resection is the only known cure. Recent case series report stabilisation of unresectable residues with CDK4/6 inhibitors (Palbociclib) — not standard of care but may be considered for truly unresectable progressive GTS. Discuss with expert team.
Somatic transformation: GTS residues may de-differentiate into sarcoma or other non-GCT tissues — prognosis dismal. Aggressive surgical clearance is strongly recommended whenever feasible.

Relapse Treatment

Germinoma relapse · NGGCT relapse · Full restaging mandatory · Expert consultation required
Germinoma Relapse — Restaging & Treatment
Full restaging mandatory: Brain + spine MRI, serum AND CSF markers + cytology, before any treatment decision.
Germinoma Relapse — Valid Treatment Approaches (Both Equally Supported)
Option A — Standard-dose chemotherapy + re-irradiation SDC + RT
Platinum + ifosfamide-based salvage chemotherapy followed by re-irradiation to relapse site(s). Murray 2017 (UK/Germany SIOP CNS GCT 96 relapse cohort) — comparable outcomes to HDC+SCT approach.
Option B — High-dose chemo + stem cell transplant ± re-irradiation HDC + SCT
Melphalan-based or thiotepa-based HDC + SCT. Kubota 2018: platinum + ifosfamide reinduction → melphalan HD-SCT — most patients avoided re-irradiation. Callec 2020: HDC+SCT results comparable to SDC+RT.
Prognosis: Relapsed germinoma — still curable in most cases with appropriate salvage. Rare event (<10% relapse rate with current first-line therapy). Discuss all relapsed cases with national and European expert teams (see ESCP Appendix K).
NGGCT Relapse — Restaging & Treatment
Poor prognosis: Relapsed NGGCT — only 14% 5y OS in historical series (Murray 2017). Thiotepa-based HDC + SCT is mandatory for curative intent.
NGGCT Relapse — Curative-Intent Strategy
Mandatory: Thiotepa-based HDC + SCT Curative intent only
Callec 2020 (n=44 relapsed GCT including 25 NGGCT): confirms HDC+SCT absolutely necessary for curative approach. Surgery and re-irradiation where feasible — optimal fields/doses/timing at relapse not established given small numbers. Discuss all cases with national + European experts.
Restaging: Symptomatic, radiological, OR marker-detected relapse → full restaging (brain + spine MRI, serum + CSF markers + cytology) before treatment.
⭐ Expert consultation: All relapsed intracranial malignant GCT patients MUST be discussed with national GCT expert teams and with SIOPE BTG CNS GCT Working Group if needed. Contact details in ESCP Appendix K (Frappaz, Murray, Nicholson, Alapetite, Ajithkumar et al.).
